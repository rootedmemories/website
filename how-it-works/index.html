How Rooted Memories works

Build your family archive one person and one memory at a time.

Rooted Memories starts with your family tree. Each person has their own place, photographs and stories are added around them, and relatives can contribute privately to the same archive. You do not need to build everything at once. The archive grows naturally as your family adds to it.

The structure is simple: people first, memories around them, family building it together.

The process

How to build your archive.

01

Create your family tree

Start by adding the people who make up your family. Each person has their own place from the beginning, giving the archive a clear structure before the photographs even start to grow.

02

Add photographs, captions and stories

Upload the memories that matter and keep them connected to the people they belong to. Add captions when there is something worth remembering beyond what can be seen in the photograph itself.

03

Invite your family

Relatives can join the private tree and contribute their own photographs and memories. Different people hold different parts of the family story, so the archive becomes richer when those pieces are brought together.

04

Keep building over time

There is no finish line. Add memories when they happen, return to older photographs when you have time, and let the archive develop gradually alongside your family.

Why people come first

The family tree is not decoration. It is the filing system.

Most digital photo collections start with files and then ask you to organise them.

Rooted Memories starts with the family instead. Each person already has a place, so memories can naturally build around the people whose lives they belong to.

That keeps the archive understandable as it grows and reduces the need for complicated folder structures.

Built together

No single person remembers the whole family story.

One relative may hold the old photographs. Another remembers the names. Someone else remembers the story behind the picture.

Rooted Memories gives those separate pieces somewhere to meet. Family members contribute to the same private archive rather than leaving family history scattered across separate devices and accounts.

What it becomes

The archive becomes more useful as the years pass.

A new tree may begin with a handful of people and a few photographs. That is enough.

As memories accumulate, individual profiles begin to form clearer records of people's lives. Childhoods develop. Relationships become visible. Older generations gain more context.

Eventually, the archive becomes something a child can open years later and understand rather than simply a collection of files they inherited.

Why it stays clear

A growing archive should not become a more complicated archive.

Rooted Memories is deliberately built around a small number of understandable ideas.

Family tree. People. Photographs. Captions. Stories.

The archive can become extremely deep without the experience needing to become difficult to navigate.

Start small

You do not need to organise your entire family history on day one.

Start with yourself, your children or your closest relatives. Add a few photographs. Invite one family member.

A serious family archive does not need to be built in a weekend. In fact, it is more useful when it becomes part of family life and develops naturally over years.

Start with the people. Let the family history grow around them.

Build a private family archive where photographs, stories and relatives remain connected in one clear place.
