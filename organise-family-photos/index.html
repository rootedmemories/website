Organising family photos

Make something beautiful from the photos your family already has.

You probably already have everything you need to start. Photos of the kids, holidays, birthdays, grandparents, old favourites and all the ordinary days in between. Rooted Memories gives you a simple way to pick out the ones you love and build them around the people in your family.

You do not need to sort your whole camera roll. Start with the photos you would actually want to sit down and look through again.

01 Why collections become difficult

Start small. There is no big sorting job.

Pick a person and add a few favourites. Your baby's first weeks. Mum and Dad when they were younger. A holiday everyone still talks about. A family photo that has been passed around for years.

That is enough. You can add more whenever you feel like it. The point is to make something you enjoy using, not give yourself another job.

02 Why context matters

Add the little bits you would hate to forget.

Sometimes the photo is enough. Sometimes there is a brilliant story behind it. Add a caption when you want to, tag the people in it, or leave it exactly as it is.

A few words from the person who was actually there can be better than a perfectly organised folder ever could be.

A practical approach

A nice way to build your family collection.

01

Choose a few people to begin with

Pick a few people you naturally want to add first. It could be your children, parents, grandparents or simply whoever you have the best photos of. There is no need to build the whole tree before you begin.

02

Add photos to the people they belong with

When you add a photo, connect it to the family members in it. The same photo can sit naturally across more than one person, so everyone gets a gallery that actually feels like their life.

03

Use captions for the good stuff

Write what you would say if you were showing the photo to someone beside you. Where you were, what made you laugh, what the kids called that toy, or why everyone still talks about that day.

04

Invite family to add theirs

Your family will have photos you have never seen. Invite them in and let them add their own favourites, captions and memories rather than sending everything to one person to sort.

05

Create history as you go

Once you have started, just keep adding to it as you take new photos. A birthday, a holiday, the first day of school or an ordinary afternoon can be added while it is still fresh. Over time, each person's gallery grows naturally with them.

Organising by person

It is much nicer to browse a person than a folder.

A folder called “Summer 2026” is useful. Opening your daughter's place and seeing her birthdays, holidays, grandparents and everyday moments together feels completely different.

You do not have to decide whether a photo belongs under Mum, Dad or the kids. If they are all in it, it can belong with all of them.

That means browsing the family feels natural. Tap a person and their gallery is there.

Older photographs

Old photos fit in just as easily as new ones.

If your parents or grandparents have boxes of old photos, they do not need a separate system. Scan the good ones and add them to the same people and family tree.

Better still, sit with them and let them tell you what they remember. You might get names, places, nicknames or stories you had never heard before.

And if nobody knows the exact year, that is fine. Add what you know and move on.

Captions and stories

A few words can make a photo much more personal.

“Dad fell in the pool five minutes after this.” “Her favourite dress for about six months.” “First night in the new house.”

That sort of thing is enough. The best captions often sound like the family member who wrote them, not a history book.

Everyone has a different piece of the family.

Mum has one set of photos. Your sister has another. Grandad has the old ones. Someone always seems to have the picture everybody else thought was lost. Put them together and everyone gets a better view of the family than they could build on their own.

A few ideas

There is no right way to do it.

Make a gallery for a child as they grow

Add a handful of favourites every so often and their gallery slowly becomes a lovely record of childhood without you having to sit down and make one later.

Keep the moments that feel like your family

Not every photo needs to mark a major occasion. Family traditions, familiar places, favourite holidays and the moments you naturally come back to all deserve a place too.

Keep adding as life moves on

The collection does not have to stop once your older photos are organised. Add new memories as they happen and you gradually create a history of your family while you are living it.

Let grandparents build their own corner

Give older relatives somewhere to add what they care about. Their photos do not have to be “old history”. They are simply their memories, in the same family as yours.

Frequently asked questions

Organising family photographs

What is the best way to organise family photos?

There is no single perfect system, but the collection should remain understandable to somebody other than the person organising it. Keeping photographs connected to the people in them, while preserving useful dates and captions, gives family memories a structure that can continue making sense over time.

Should family photos be organised by date or person?

Dates are useful for chronology, while people are useful for identity. You can use both. Organising around people provides the core structure, while dates help show how that person's life and family history developed.

How should I organise old family photographs?

Identify people first while relatives who recognise them are available. Then record approximate dates, locations and short stories where known. Scan physical photographs carefully and preserve the original files as well as any organised viewing copies.

Do I need to organise every photograph I own?

No. You definitely do not need every screenshot, duplicate or accidental photo. Start with photographs that record people, relationships, events and moments worth preserving.

Why are captions important?

Captions preserve information that may not be visible in the image. Even one sentence explaining who was there, what happened or why the photograph mattered can significantly increase its value to somebody looking at it decades later.
