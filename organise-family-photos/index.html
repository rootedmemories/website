Organise family photos

Make a collection
you can make sense of.

Turn an unsorted batch into photographs you can identify. Begin with selection, then add the names, dates and notes that make each picture easier to find and understand.

Work through a batch ↓

Three groups to work with.

  • Ready to organiseClear photographs with people or occasions you recognise.
  • Needs a second lookUnknown faces, uncertain dates or several similar copies.
  • Outside this collectionReceipts, screenshots and unrelated files.
A repeatable method

Finish one batch.
Then choose the next.

Keep the task small enough to review properly. This method works for a folder of digital pictures or scans from an older album.

  1. 01

    Choose one manageable batch

    Pick a single album, envelope or event. Keep that group together while you work, rather than mixing it with every photograph you own.

  2. 02

    Separate the keepers from the questions

    Make a first selection of clear, useful photographs. Set uncertain copies aside for review, including near-duplicates, unidentified faces and pictures that need a better scan.

  3. 03

    Identify the people and places

    Write full names where you can. Ask relatives about unfamiliar faces and record who supplied an identification, especially if accounts differ.

  4. 04

    Record dates without guessing

    Distinguish the date a picture was taken from the date it was scanned or copied. If only a decade or year is known, put that estimate in the caption and mark it as approximate.

  5. 05

    Check the batch before moving on

    Read the captions as if you had never seen the pictures. Check that names are understandable and uncertainties are labelled. Keep the originals and a separate copy of your digital files.

Writing a useful caption

Be specific about
what you know.

“Summer holiday” is a start. Names, a location and one remembered detail give the next reader much more to work with.

Keep estimates visible. “Probably 1986” is more useful than an exact date nobody can verify.

“Eileen and Peter outside the caravan in Tenby, probably summer 1986. Peter had just learned to ride his bike.”
People and place
Eileen and Peter · Tenby

When the details are unclear

Leave room
for uncertainty.

What if I cannot identify someone?

Write down what is known and ask relatives separately. Keep a suggested name marked as unconfirmed until you have stronger evidence.

Should I organise by date, person or occasion?

Use dates for sequence, names for identification and occasions for context. A caption can hold all three, so you do not have to rely on a single folder name.

Do I need to delete duplicates first?

Set obvious duplicates aside from your working batch. Compare resolution, cropping and any handwritten information before deciding which copies to retain. Keep your source files until you are satisfied with the result.
