Organising family photos

A simple way to organise the family photos you want to keep.

Most families already have thousands of photographs spread across phones, old albums and different relatives. You do not need to organise every one of them. Start with the photographs that matter to you, connect them to the people in your family and add the details you would like to remember.

There is no need to sort your entire camera roll before you begin. Start with a few photographs you already care about and allow the collection to grow over time.

01 Getting started

Begin with a small part of the family rather than trying to organise everything at once.

Choose someone you naturally want to begin with and add a few photographs you already enjoy looking at. It might be your children, your parents, your grandparents or simply the person whose photographs you already have to hand.

There is no need to complete the whole family tree first. You can add more people and more photographs whenever you want to.

02 Keeping context

Keep the details that help a photograph make sense later.

A photograph may show who was there, but it will not always explain where it was taken, what was happening or why the moment mattered.

When there is something worth remembering, add a short caption, check the date and connect the photograph to the people in it. A few useful details are often enough.

A practical approach

Build the collection gradually as you find photographs worth keeping.

01

Choose a few family members to begin with

Start with the people you already have photographs of. There is no need to build every branch of the family tree before adding memories.

02

Connect photographs to the people in them

When you add a photograph, attach it to the family members who appear in it. If several people are in the same photograph, it can appear naturally within each of their galleries.

03

Add useful captions when you know the story

You do not need to write much. A sentence about where you were, who was there or what was happening may be more useful years later than a long description.

04

Invite relatives to contribute their own photographs

Different relatives will have different parts of the family's collection. Inviting them to contribute means those photographs and stories do not all have to pass through one person.

05

Keep adding new memories as life moves on

Once the collection has been started, new photographs can simply be added as they are taken. Birthdays, holidays, school days and ordinary family moments gradually become part of the same history.

Organising by person

Organising photographs around people makes the collection easier to understand.

Folders organised by year or event can be useful, but they only provide one way of looking through a collection.

When photographs are connected to the people in them, each family member gradually develops a gallery of their own life. Their childhood, holidays, birthdays, parents, grandparents and ordinary everyday photographs can all be viewed together.

The same photograph can still belong with several people, so there is no need to decide which single folder it should live in.

Older photographs

Older family photographs can sit alongside the ones you take today.

If your parents or grandparents have boxes of old photographs, you can scan the ones you want to keep and add them to the same family collection.

Where possible, go through them with relatives who recognise the people and places. They may be able to provide names, approximate dates, locations or stories that would otherwise be difficult to recover later.

If the exact date is unknown, that is not a problem. Record what you know and leave the rest as it is.

Captions and stories

Captions are most useful when they sound like the person who remembers the moment.

“Dad fell in the pool five minutes after this.” “She wore that dress everywhere.” “First night in the new house.”

A caption does not need to sound formal or historically important. Its value is simply in preserving something about the photograph that somebody in the family knew at the time.

A family collection is usually spread across more than one person.

Your parents may have one set of photographs, your brothers or sisters another, and grandparents may have older photographs that nobody else has. Bringing those collections together gives the family a much fuller record than one person could create alone.

Different ways to use it

There is no single way a family archive has to be built.

Keep a record of your children as they grow

Adding a small number of photographs every so often creates a record of childhood gradually, without having to organise years of photographs later.

Keep the ordinary family photographs as well as the important occasions

Weddings and birthdays matter, but familiar places, family traditions, holidays and ordinary days often become just as enjoyable to look back on.

Continue adding to the archive as new memories are made

Family history is not limited to old photographs. The photographs being taken now can become part of the same collection and remain connected to the people in them.

Let older relatives contribute what matters to them

Parents and grandparents may want to add different photographs and stories from the ones you would choose yourself. Giving them their own place to contribute helps preserve their view of the family as well.

Frequently asked questions

Organising family photographs

What is the best way to organise family photos?

There is no single best method, but the collection should remain understandable to somebody other than the person who organised it. Connecting photographs to the people in them, while keeping useful dates and captions, gives the collection a structure that can continue making sense over time.

Should family photos be organised by date or person?

Both can be useful. Dates provide chronology, while organising photographs around people makes it easier to follow an individual's life and their place within the wider family. Rooted Memories combines the two by organising memories around people while retaining the dates associated with them.

How should I organise old family photographs?

Start by identifying the people in them while relatives who recognise them are still able to help. Record approximate dates, locations and short stories where they are known, then scan the photographs you would like to preserve and connect them to the relevant family members.

Do I need to organise every photograph I own?

No. It is usually easier to begin with photographs that matter to you and gradually add more over time. Screenshots, duplicates and photographs you do not care about do not need to become part of the collection.

Why are captions useful on family photos?

Captions preserve information that may not be obvious from the photograph itself. Even a short explanation of where it was taken, who was there or what was happening can make the photograph easier for somebody else in the family to understand years later.
