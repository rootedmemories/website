Choosing and organising

Keep the collection useful.
Keep the task manageable.

01 / Choosing photographs

You do not need every photograph.

Begin with a few pictures you already enjoy looking at. Choose a person or part of the family you have photographs of, rather than trying to sort the entire camera roll. Screenshots, duplicates and unrelated files do not need to become part of the collection.

02 / Organising by person

Give each picture a connection.

Dates and occasions help you place a photograph in time. Connecting it to the people in it gives you another way to look through the collection. In Rooted Memories, the same photograph can appear in the galleries of several family members.

03 / Adding context

Write what the picture cannot tell you.

A short caption can record where you were, what was happening or why you remember the moment. It does not need to be formal or lengthy. Keep the details you know, in the words you would naturally use.

Older photographs

Find out what
your relatives remember.

Scan the printed photographs you want to keep. Where possible, look through them with relatives who recognise the faces and places. They may know names, approximate dates or details you would otherwise miss.

If the exact date is unknown, record what you know. You do not need to fill in every detail before adding the photograph.
